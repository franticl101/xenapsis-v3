Your result says **63% Introversion**. Almost everyone reads that as "I am 63% introverted". That is not what it means, and the real meaning is considerably more useful.

## What the number measures

Every dimension is scored from a set of statements, half worded in each direction. Your answers are converted to a signed score and totalled. That total is then expressed as a position on a scale running from one pole to the other.

**50% is an exact tie.** It is the centre, not the bottom.

So the percentage measures **how consistently you answered in one direction**, not how much of a trait you have. A result of 63% Introversion means your answers leaned 63% of the way from dead centre toward the introverted end.

This is why the scale cannot be read as a quantity. There is no such thing as having 63% of the available introversion. There is only leaning toward one end, by some distance.

## Preference strength, and why we report it separately

Because 50% is the midpoint, the distance from 50 is the number that carries the information. A result of 63% is 13 points from centre — a mild lean. A result of 94% is 44 points from centre — a strong, stable one.

On this site that distance is reported directly as **preference strength**, scaled so that 0% means an exact tie and 100% means you answered every item on that dimension at the extreme, consistently.

| Strength | Reading |
|---|---|
| Under 12% | A near tie. You use both sides about equally. |
| 12–35% | A slight preference. |
| 35–60% | A moderate preference. |
| 60–85% | A clear preference. |
| Above 85% | A very strong preference. |

## Why a near tie is real information

People are often disappointed by a near tie, as though the test failed to produce an answer. It did produce an answer: **both, depending on context.**

That is a genuine finding and a common one. Most people sit near the middle on at least one dimension, because the underlying scores are roughly normally distributed — the bulk of any population is near the centre, and the poles are sparsely populated.

If you scored 52% Thinking, the accurate description is not "you are a Thinker". It is that you weigh logic and human impact at roughly equal strength, and which one leads probably depends on the situation. That is worth knowing, and a bare four-letter code destroys it entirely.

## This explains most "the test got me wrong" complaints

Two things follow directly.

**Different tests give you different letters.** If a dimension sits at 51%, the letter reported is close to a coin toss. A different instrument with slightly different wording will land on the other side about as often as not. Neither test is broken; the question simply has no stable answer for you.

**Your type seems to change.** Same cause. Retake a test in a different mood or a different job and a near-tie dimension will flip. Dimensions where you scored above roughly 70% almost never do.

Which is the practical rule: **trust your strong letters, hold your weak ones loosely.**

## How to use this when reading a type profile

Read the profile for your strong dimensions closely and the parts driven by your weak ones sceptically.

If you came out INFJ with 88% Introversion, 74% Intuition, 61% Feeling and 9% Judging, then the J is nearly meaningless — you are functionally somewhere between INFJ and INFP, and you will likely recognise yourself in both. Read both, and take the parts that land.

That is a perfectly legitimate outcome. It is also invisible if all you are given is four letters.
